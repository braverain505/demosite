Student life

Busy days, warm evenings, real belonging

Learning is the centre — but the days around it are just as carefully built: clubs, houses, sport and a boarding house that feels like family.

A day at EIS

The bell-to-bell rhythm

Illustrated moments from an ordinary school day — the structure is real, the pictures are a production illustration rather than a photograph.

07:30 — Gates

Arrival & registration

Uniform checked, names ticked, bags down — then the walk past the lawns to the first classroom. Boarders join from breakfast in the hall.

08:00 — Assembly

Assembly & first lessons

Hymns, notices, a word of the day. Then subject lessons through break — and in the labs, from Primary, the very first goggles go on.

12:30 — Lunch

Dining halls, two of them

Hot lunch in the two dining halls, with table manners and very little food wasted. Boarders and older pupils set the table for the younger ones.

15:30 — Games & clubs

Clubs and the field

Football, debate, journalism, chess, art, science club. Inter-house points are at stake and careers are quietly decided.

17:00 — Boarders

Evening study & supper

For those who live in: supervised evening prep, supper in the hall, and lights-out handled with kindness. Day pupils head home with homework done.

Belonging

Houses, prefects and clubs

Every EIS pupil belongs to a house the day they arrive — and can grow into leadership from the next term.

Four houses

Point every pupil to a house on arrival — inter-house debate, sport and prize days give the year its friendly rivalries. (House names confirmed by the office.)

Prefects & Parliament

Senior pupils take real responsibility — prefect duties, house captaincy and a School Parliament that meets to make things better.

Clubs & societies

Debate and journalism, science club and chess, art and football — an afternoon timetable that teaches as much as lessons do.

Boarding

A home away from home

For Junior and Senior boarders, the house is arranged around care: a settled routine, staff who know each child by name, dinner in the hall and evening prep supervised.

  • Cared-for residential life with dedicated pastoral staff
  • Evening supervised prep built into the week
  • Two dining halls and a hot supper every night
  • Quiet, safe and structured — with visits home on weekends
